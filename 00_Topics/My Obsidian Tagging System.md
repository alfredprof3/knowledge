> ---
> 
> **What your folder structure is doing right**
> 
> Your folders are clean and logical. Three top-level areas — knowledge, teaching, project — is exactly the right level of simplicity. The subfolders make sense too. This structure answers one question well: _where is the note stored?_ Don't change it.
> 
> **What your current tags are failing to do**
> 
> Your 349 flat tags like `#figma`, `#Branding`, `#ChatGPT` answer a different question — _what is this note about?_ — but because they're all the same "shape," they give you no useful grouping in the dashboard. Everything ends up in one undifferentiated pile. The tag-navigator can't create meaningful columns from flat tags.
> 
> **The key insight going forward**
> 
> Folders answer WHERE. Tags should answer WHAT TYPE and FOR WHAT CONTEXT. Used together, they become powerful. A note about Figma stored in `00_knowledge/00_Tools/` can simultaneously be:
> 
> - A design tool (`topic/design`)
> - Useful for teaching (`for/teaching`)
> - Specifically for your InCrea students (`for/increa`)
> 
> That cross-reference view is exactly what the dashboard was built to show.
> 
> ---
> 
> **My proposed tag group system — 4 groups only**
> 
> I'm keeping this deliberately minimal. You can always add more later, but complexity kills habits.
> 
> **`type/`** — What kind of note is it? `type/tool` · `type/howto` · `type/reference` · `type/topic` · `type/case-study` · `type/tip`
> 
> **`topic/`** — What subject does it cover? `topic/design` · `topic/marketing` · `topic/photography` · `topic/animation` · `topic/programming` · `topic/ux`· `topic/branding` · `topic/ai` · `topic/ecommerce` · `topic/semiotics` _(and others as needed)_
> 
> **`for/`** — Who or what context is this for? `for/teaching` · `for/increa` · `for/personal`
> 
> **`school/`** — Which institution? _(teaching notes only)_ `school/henbord` · `school/humanitas`
> 
> ---
> 
> **What this looks like in practice**
> 
> Your note on the twelve principles of animation gets: `type/topic` + `topic/animation` + `for/teaching`
> 
> Your note on Figma as a tool gets: `type/tool` + `topic/design` + `for/teaching` + `for/increa`
> 
> A student attendance sheet for Humanitas gets: `type/reference` + `topic/photography` + `for/teaching` + `school/humanitas`
> 
> Now the dashboard shows meaningful columns: all your design tools together, all your teaching materials together, everything related to InCrea together — and notes appear in multiple columns simultaneously based on their context.
> 
> ---
> 
> **Migration strategy — gradual, not all at once**
> 
> Don't try to retag 270 notes in one sitting. That's a recipe for abandoning the whole system.

> **Going forward:** Every new note gets tagged with the new system from day one.
> 
> **Over time:** When you open an old note for any reason — to use it in class, to share it, to update it — retag it then. Opportunistic migration. Within a few months, your most-used notes will all be properly tagged without a single dedicated cleanup session.

> Good instinct to think about this — and my answer is: **don't create a new `00_personal` folder.** Here's why.
> 
> Remember the principle we set up: folders answer WHERE something lives based on its subject matter, while tags answer WHAT TYPE it is and WHO it's FOR. If you create a `00_personal` folder, you'd be using a folder to express the same thing the `for/personal` tag already expresses — and you'd be right back to mixing the two systems, which is what caused the original mess.
> 
> **My recommendation: put it in `00_knowledge/00_Topics`.**
> 
> Think about what this note actually _is_: it's a write-up explaining a system — how you organize, classify, and find your notes. That's conceptually the same kind of thing as your "twelve principles of animation" note or your "principles of graphic design" note — research and thinking on a subject, written for future reference. The subject here just happens to be _note-taking itself_. The `topic/note-taking` tag you already applied confirms this is the right category.
> 
> **The general rule going forward:** when deciding _where_ a note goes, ask "what is this fundamentally about?" — a tool, a topic, a cheatsheet, a case study — and use the matching `00_knowledge` subfolder, regardless of who it's for. The `for/` tag handles the "who it's for" question separately. That keeps the two systems doing two different jobs, which is exactly the point.
> 
> So: save it as something like `00_knowledge/00_Topics/My Obsidian Tagging System.md`, keep the three tags you already added, and you're done.
